# FIA&CO presentation and study method

Use a source-grounded context-stacking workflow with NotebookLM when preparing the owner to explain FIA&CO to companies, accelerators, banks, or investors. This is a working method attributed online to an MIT student, not an official MIT academic method; refer to it as **MIT/context stacking** only to match the owner's chosen shorthand.

## Source pack

Build a dated source inventory containing only current, relevant material:

- verified live product and demo links;
- current repository and architecture summaries;
- test results and pilot evidence;
- business model and financial assumptions;
- legal, security, and privacy boundaries;
- market and competitor research with citations;
- letters of interest or attributable company feedback;
- roadmap and requested use of funds.

Exclude credentials, private keys, real personal identifiers, regulated payment data, and obsolete drafts. Mark each source with its date, owner, status, and authority.

## Reduction layers

Produce these layers from the same evidence:

1. **Landscape:** one map of the problem, users, workflow, value, risks, and evidence.
2. **Executive brief:** two pages separating live, testing, prototype, and planned capabilities.
3. **Ten-slide deck:** problem, solution, workflow, security, market, validation, business model, roadmap, team, and ask.
4. **Two-minute explanation:** plain language with one concrete synthetic example.
5. **Thirty-second opening:** problem, distinctive solution, and requested next step.
6. **Active recall:** questions asked one at a time, scored for correctness, clarity, and unsupported claims.

NotebookLM should be instructed to answer only from the provided source pack and cite the source supporting each material claim. Resolve contradictions in the source pack before rehearsing.

## Audience variants

- **Potential customer:** operational problem, time saved, responsibilities, pilot scope, security, and next step.
- **Accelerator:** innovation, team, scalability, validation plan, milestones, and use of funds.
- **Investor:** market, defensibility, traction, unit economics, regulatory path, financing ask, and dilution assumptions.
- **Bank or public lender:** legal entity, own funds, repayment capacity, contracts, cash flow, risks, and safeguards.

Never reuse one generic pitch unchanged across these audiences.

## Rehearsal

Challenge weak points rather than memorizing promotional wording. Include questions about liability, failed delivery, disputes, data breaches, unauthorized access, payment regulation, customer acquisition, margins, competition, and why three-party coordination is necessary.

When an answer exceeds the evidence, replace it with an honest boundary and the next validation milestone. The final material must be understandable on mobile and printable without relying on animations or images.
